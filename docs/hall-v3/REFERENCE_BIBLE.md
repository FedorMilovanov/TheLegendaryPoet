# Hall v3 — Reference Bible

Status: **Gate 1 / active** under `TLP-HALL-001`.

This document converts museum research into design evidence. It is not a mood board and it does not approve a building form. The machine-readable counterpart is [`reference-bible.json`](reference-bible.json); if prose and machine state disagree, the gate fails rather than silently choosing one.

## Reading rule

Every reference must answer one primary design problem and record:

- **TAKE** — a transferable principle worth testing;
- **AVOID** — the literal form, motif or failure mode we must not copy;
- **WHY** — how the reference changes a Hall decision;
- **SOURCE** — an inspectable institutional or conservation source.

A beautiful image without a design problem is not Hall evidence.

## Current non-decisions

The Reference Bible does **not** approve:

- a dome or rotunda;
- four wings or any fixed wing count;
- a fixed era topology;
- a long nave;
- FPS/free-walk;
- a black-and-gold niche system;
- per-poet visual archetypes from legacy `poetMuseumMeta`;
- any documentary image for production use merely because its museum page is public.

Those remain blocked until later gates produce independent evidence.

## R1 — British Museum Great Court

**Primary problem:** circulation  
**Secondary:** sightline, scale, camera

**TAKE**

- A central space is valuable when it distributes people and exposes multiple onward choices.
- Changing vistas during movement are more useful than one static symmetrical hero view.

**AVOID**

- Do not copy the glass roof or circular Reading Room.
- Do not create a monumental empty centre that exists only for spectacle.

**WHY**

The Hall may use an orientation court, but it must prove route choice, destination visibility and a reason to exist with all UI hidden.

**SOURCE**  
https://www.britishmuseum.org/about-us/british-museum-story/architecture/great-court

## R2 — Louvre Cour Marly / Cour Puget

**Primary problem:** daylight  
**Secondary:** sightline, scale, artificial light

**TAKE**

- Tiered levels can create hierarchy and varied views.
- Overhead daylight can contribute to spatial identity when it is controlled rather than romanticized.

**AVOID**

- Do not fill a literary museum with monumental sculpture simply because the precedent is monumental.
- Do not assume a glass-roof look is itself a quality signal.

**WHY**

A future Hall can use overhead-light logic, but the greybox must first work in neutral light and sensitive paper display must remain separately controlled.

**SOURCE**  
https://www.louvre.fr/en/explore/the-palace/shining-new-light-on-sculpture

## R3 — The Met, Michael C. Rockefeller Wing

**Primary problem:** sightline  
**Secondary:** circulation, scale, portrait display, camera

**TAKE**

- Use a strong trajectory and anchor sightlines for orientation.
- Alternate large establishing spaces with smaller focus rooms for close looking.
- Let collection needs shape display without turning each zone into themed scenery.

**AVOID**

- Do not repeat equal bays with equal visual weight.
- Do not borrow regional motifs unrelated to Russian literary material.

**WHY**

The Hall needs a spatial hierarchy of reveal → destination → intimate poet encounter, not a corridor of interchangeable niches.

**SOURCE**  
https://www.metmuseum.org/de/press-releases/mcrw-opening-english-2025

## R4 — National Portrait Gallery chronology / floor plans

**Primary problem:** circulation  
**Secondary:** signage, accessibility

**TAKE**

- Chronology can reduce navigation complexity.
- A visitor should be able to focus on one historical period without traversing the entire collection.

**AVOID**

- Do not translate chronology into a predetermined four-wing building.
- Do not make later periods reachable only through a complete linear journey.

**WHY**

Chronology is a possible information architecture, not an architectural command.

**SOURCES**  
https://www.npg.org.uk/visit/floor-plans/  
https://www.npg.org.uk/visit/your-visit/

## R5 — National Portrait Gallery wall design

**Primary problem:** portrait display  
**Secondary:** material, artificial light

**TAKE**

- Background material, reflectance and colour should be chosen to improve the actual portrait under controlled light.
- Evaluate portrait, surface and light as one optical composition.

**AVOID**

- Do not assign every poet a loud bespoke colour/material world.
- Do not default to metallic black walls and glowing gold frames.

**WHY**

The portrait is the exhibit anchor. Architecture supports its reading rather than advertising a visual theme.

**SOURCE**  
https://www.npg.org.uk/blog/walls-of-new-npg

## R6 — Getty Museum Lighting Research

**Primary problem:** document display  
**Secondary:** daylight, artificial light

**TAKE**

- Lighting for works on paper is a conservation decision as well as a cinematic one.
- Separate the readable circulation environment from fragile-object display lighting.

**AVOID**

- Do not apply one dramatic exposure recipe to portrait, paper, photo and architecture.
- Do not compensate for weak composition with brighter spots or post effects.

**WHY**

Hall light design must contain controlled document pockets instead of making the whole route dark or the documents too bright.

**SOURCE**  
https://www.getty.edu/projects/museum-lighting-research/

## R7 — Smithsonian Accessible Exhibition Design

**Primary problem:** accessibility  
**Secondary:** circulation, signage, sightline

**TAKE**

- Accessibility dimensions are greybox geometry inputs.
- Route lighting must support obstacle and floor legibility and avoid glare or abrupt bright/dark pools.
- A visitor stopping at an exhibit must not necessarily block the circulation path.

**AVOID**

- Do not treat accessibility as a late UI patch.
- Do not optimize the main route down to a narrow one-person corridor merely because it satisfies an absolute minimum.

**WHY**

The first Blender blockout must prove clearances and route legibility before look-development begins.

**SOURCE**  
https://www.sifacilities.si.edu/sites/default/files/Files/Accessibility/accessible-exhibition-design1.pdf

## R8 — British Museum accessibility

**Primary problem:** accessibility  
**Secondary:** circulation, signage, digital boundary

**TAKE**

- Provide direct accessible routes, rest equivalents and multiple information modalities.
- Treat non-3D content access as an equal route, not a reduced fallback.

**AVOID**

- Do not require free locomotion to reach information.
- Do not make a future Canvas the sole owner of navigation or interpretation.

**WHY**

Hall can be immersive without making immersion mandatory. Canonical content remains reachable through accessible DOM.

**SOURCES**  
https://www.britishmuseum.org/visit/accessibility-museum  
https://www.britishmuseum.org/accessibility-statement

## R9 — Canadian Conservation Institute, paper objects

**Primary problem:** document display  
**Secondary:** artificial light, daylight

**TAKE**

- Treat light exposure to sensitive paper as cumulative.
- Roughly 50 lux or as low as practical is an evidence guide for sensitive paper, with object-specific exposure management.
- Rotation, limited exposure and facsimiles may be legitimate preservation tools.

**AVOID**

- Do not expose manuscripts to route-level theatrical lighting.
- Do not confuse a facsimile strategy with permission to fabricate a historical original.

**WHY**

Document display must have a controlled-light mode and explicit provenance/rights status.

**SOURCES**  
https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/preventive-conservation/guidelines-collections/paper-objects.html  
https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/conservation-preservation-publications/canadian-conservation-institute-notes/storing-works-paper.html

## R10 — All-Russian Museum of A. S. Pushkin

**Primary problem:** portrait display  
**Secondary:** document display, sightline, signage

**TAKE**

- A dominant Pushkin portrait can establish the exhibit while smaller contextual portraits/documents form a supporting evidence field.
- The first vertical slice should be object-led and legible before decorative architecture is added.

**AVOID**

- Do not assume the public museum page grants reproduction rights to its object images.
- Do not copy the museum room literally without testing it against Hall circulation/camera requirements.

**WHY**

Pushkin gives the project a concrete exhibit hierarchy to test while rights clearance remains a separate gate.

**SOURCE**  
https://www.museumpushkin.ru/vserossijskij_muzej_a._s._pushkina/muzej_pushkin._zhizn_i_tvorchestvo.html

## Data authority boundary

Reference work must not silently reactivate legacy visual metadata.

- **Canonical after normal product validation:** poet ids, names, dates, portraits and published content in `src/data/library/*`.
- **Reverify before any optional semantic/navigation use:** `src/data/poetConnections.ts`.
- **Legacy visual-concept candidate, not Hall authority:** `src/data/poetMuseumMeta.ts`.
- **UI-only unless art direction explicitly approves another use:** `src/data/epochColors.ts`.

The presence of a file under `src` is not evidence that Hall v3 should inherit its visual model.
