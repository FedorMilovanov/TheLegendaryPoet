## 1. Chapter function

This chapter must explain why the “tavern poet” image mattered to the 1923–1924 biography without repeating the independent article.

It must hold four truths together:

1. the role was consciously performed and commercially recognisable;
2. cafes, evenings and book titles created a market/publicity system;
3. real dependence, disorder and harm existed beyond performance;
4. poetry did not become strong because alcohol was creatively necessary.

## 2. Governing claim IDs

- `YES2-I005` — the hooligan persona was partly constructed but not only marketing.
- `YES2-I006` — cafe accounts do not prove personal consumption of every item.
- `YES2-L001` — distinct legal cases must not be fused.
- `YES2-M001` — self-accusation/confession does not automatically equal repentance.
- `YES2-M005` — dependence matters causally but does not erase responsibility.

## 4. Preferred opening

Use the physical book/project history rather than an invented drunken scene.

**Possible opening:**

> `Москва кабацкая` became a durable image before it became a stable physical book. The title circulated through projects, readings and public expectation; the Leningrad edition appeared only in July 1924.

This immediately separates role/history from anecdotal spectacle.

### Paragraph 4 — performance does not cancel dependence

Use:

- real police/legal incidents;
- witness reports classified by time/interest;
- letters/poems naming disorder;
- repeated attempts at treatment/restraint.

**Boundary:**

Do not call all behaviour “PR”. Do not use one medical label to explain every event.

### Paragraph 5 — September 1923 case as one example

Compact sequence:

- 15 September incident;
- 16 September protocol;
- 11 October criminal action;
- 23 November hearing suspended because summons not served/accused absent.

**Purpose:** show document chain, not narrate spectacle.

**Boundary:**

This is not the November four-poets case and not the 1924 `Домино`/other files.

### Paragraph 6 — book body versus contents/censorship

**Facts:**

- physical July 1924 book;
- contents preserve names of removed texts;
- body/contents need physical comparison;
- authorial book list later misdates release.

**Safe formula:**

> Even a contents page can preserve a version of the book that censorship prevented readers from receiving.

### Paragraph 7 — poetry and responsibility

Required distinctions:

- intoxication is not inspiration;
- confession is not automatic repentance;
- dependence explains vulnerability but does not rename harm as freedom;
- audience complicity/market reward does not transfer all responsibility away from the poet.

**Allowed moral sentence:**

> The public may have rewarded the role, but repeated self-destruction and harm to others remained real choices inside a real dependence.

### Paragraph 8 — bridge onward

Transition toward Imagism/Benislavskaya:

> The same period that fixed the tavern mask also increased Yesenin's dependence on people who handled manuscripts, money, publishers and the practical consequences of his disorder.

This leads naturally to chapters 10–11.
