## 1. Narrative purpose

The year 1922 must not be written as one continuous scandal montage. The evidence separates at least four processes:

1. the Moscow legal/logistical preparation of the journey;
2. Berlin publishing, readings and public conflict;
3. a European route in which documented stops, reconstructed intervals and announced plans are not interchangeable;
4. the American tour, immigration suspicion, press construction and literary work.

The year also demonstrates why `published abroad`, `written abroad`, `announced abroad` and `translated abroad` must be different claim categories.

## 2. Status vocabulary

- `DOCUMENTED` — primary legal, passport, letter, contract, dated inscription or transport object.
- `PRIMARY-TEXT` — dated authorial text whose self-report remains critically read.
- `SYNCHRONOUS-PRESS` — contemporary press evidence, useful for reception but not automatically legal or complete.
- `ACADEMICALLY-RECONSTRUCTED` — scholarly interval based on multiple sources, without an exact day.
- `PLANNED-NOT-CONFIRMED` — stated intention without the next presence document.
- `ROUTE-BIN` — city/event known to belong to the tour, but not yet closed day by day.
- `MEMOIR-MAPPED` — later witness preserved with name/date/genre.
- `HOLD` — insufficient for reader-level certainty.

## 6. Standing anti-regression rules from 1922

1. **Legal marriage does not prove a single secret motive.** Passport value, public image and emotional relationship may coexist.
2. **Passport issuance is not a route.** It proves legal capacity to leave, not every later stop.
3. **A city named in future tense remains a plan.** The Hague, Brussels and Rome need follow-up evidence.
4. **A photograph requires a representation chain.** A dated published photostat with unknown original is not labelled as a directly held original.
5. **Contemporary press may preserve a rumour.** Synchrony increases value for reception history, not truth of a false divorce claim.
6. **Published abroad is not written abroad.** Book contents and authorial labour must be separately dated.
7. **Immigration scrutiny is not deportation.** Legal terminology requires a legal document.
8. **Tour advertising is not performance completion.** Each city needs programme/report/letter/transport confirmation.
9. **One angry or ecstatic letter is not an annual average.** Personal correspondence is a dated state and rhetorical act.
10. **Authorial geographic hyperbole is not a route register.** “Almost all states” remains unproven.

## 7. Narrative consequences for Part II

### Chapter order

The foreign section should proceed:

1. marriage and legal preparation;
2. Berlin publishing and public conflict;
3. documented Europe with plan/stop distinctions;
4. New York arrival and political framing;
5. tour infrastructure and city bins;
6. literary work matrix;
7. transition into January–February 1923.

### Berlin must not be a scandal catalogue

The 14 May autobiography, 18 May contract, volume preparation, readings and later physical publication provide the chapter spine. The House of Arts incident belongs inside this production context, not as the whole meaning of Berlin.

### America begins with contradictory evidence

Arrival produced both fascination and suspicion. Contemporary newspapers could admire the poet's first view of the skyline while simultaneously casting the couple as political emissaries. The article should present those parallel frames rather than choose one sensational headline.
