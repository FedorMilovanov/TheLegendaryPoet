## 1. Purpose

This pass narrows the late New York chronology and tests whether the current exact formula `4 February 1923 — departure from the USA` is sufficiently supported.

It is not yet a complete American tour ledger. Cities without exact event records remain route bins, not day-level facts.

## 2. Strong anchors

### 1 October 1922 — arrival in New York / start of US period

Current academic and archival summaries consistently place the beginning of the US period on 1 October 1922.

Useful sources:

- FEБ/RVB academic comments and route references;
- NYPL Irma Duncan collection chronology: `https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19640`;
- FEБ sitemap/photo inventory includes an item explicitly described as Yesenin and Duncan arriving in New York on 1 October 1922: `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/sitemap.htm`.

**Status:** `DOCUMENTED / retain`.

### October 1922 — Carnegie Hall and tour disruption

The NYPL Irma Duncan collection chronology states that Duncan began the US tour with sold-out Carnegie Hall concerts in October 1922 and that much of the tour was cancelled because of accusations concerning her “Bolshevik tendencies”.

**What this supports:** successful initial public demand and a later politically affected tour environment.

**What it does not support:** legal deportation of Duncan or Yesenin; a finding aid chronology is not an immigration ruling.

**Status:** `ARCHIVAL-CHRONOLOGY / REQUIRE ITEM-LEVEL PROGRAMS AND PRESS`.

### 12 November 1922 — Yesenin letter from New York

Academic publication:

`https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/es6/es6-1494.htm?cmd=p`

The dated letter to Anatoly Mariengof localises Yesenin in New York and gives a primary record of his emotional state, financial expectations and view of America.

**Status:** `PRIMARY-LETTER / retain`.

**Boundary:** one bitter letter does not describe every day of the tour or prove a fixed final evaluation throughout the trip.

### November 1922 — New York book project handed to Avrahm Yarmolinsky

FEБ's author-books register records an autograph mock-up titled `Стихи и поэмы. Нью-Йорк, 1922`, handed in November 1922 to A. Yarmolinsky, head of the Slavic division of the New York Public Library, for English translation and possible publication:

`https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/es7/es7-0724.htm?cmd=2`

The register also states that surviving memoir evidence does not establish whether the translation was completed or the book published.

**Status:** `DOCUMENTED PROJECT / PUBLICATION NOT PROVEN`.

This is an important counterweight to the claim that the American period contained only scandals and no literary or publication work.

### Night of 27–28 January 1923 — reading at Mani-Leib's apartment, New York

Academic comments to `Страна Негодяев` state that four memoirists left accounts of a reading of an early version at the apartment of the Yiddish poet Mani-Leib (M. L. Braginsky) during the night of 27–28 January 1923:

- `https://rvb.ru/20vek/esenin/pss7/vol3/notes/205.html`
- `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/es3/es3-435-.htm?cmd=p`

**Status:** `MULTIPLE-MEMOIR / ACADEMICALLY-MAPPED`.

**Boundary:** four memoirs strengthen the event but do not make every quoted sentence a stenographic transcript. Each version must be mapped separately before reader-text quotation.

### Before 3 February 1923 — lost early autograph handed to V. M. Levin

The general academic comments to volume 3 state that an autograph of the first part of `Страна Негодяев` was given to V. M. Levin before 3 February 1923 in New York. The original and alleged photostats have not been located:

`https://rvb.ru/20vek/esenin/pss7/vol3/notes/000.html`

**Status:** `MEMOIR-BASED LOST OBJECT / NOT VISUALLY AVAILABLE`.

Do not illustrate the article with an unrelated manuscript while captioning it as this lost autograph.

## 3. Departure-date correction: 3 or 4 February 1923

### Evidence for 3 February

The academic comments to `Страна Негодяев` say that Yesenin left New York on 3 February 1923 and cite the `New York Sun` and `New York Globe` of that date:

- `https://rvb.ru/20vek/esenin/pss7/vol3/notes/205.html`
- `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/es3/es3-435-.htm?cmd=p`

This comment is used to reject the literal authorial date `New York, 14 February 1923` for part of the poem.

### Evidence for 4 February

A FEБ academic annotation to a memoir states that Yesenin and Duncan were in the United States from 1 October 1922 to 4 February 1923:

`https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/critics/ev2/ev2-361-.htm?cmd=p`

### Current editorial verdict

```yaml
claim: Yesenin and Duncan left the United States on exactly 4 February 1923
verdict: qualified
safe_public_formula: They left New York / ended the US stage on 3–4 February 1923; academic references differ by one day.
status: HOLD-FOR-TRANSPORT-RECORD
```

Possible explanations include departure from New York on the 3rd versus the formal sailing/US endpoint on the 4th, but this is an inference and must not be stated as fact without a passenger list, ship timetable, ticket, immigration record or exact contemporary press report.

### Required correction

The current route source map contains an exact `4 February 1923 — departure from the USA` row. Until the transport record is closed, read it together with this correction pass and use the interval `3–4 February 1923` in new prose.

## 4. Consequences for the “deportation” myth

The corrected interval still does not establish deportation.

Available evidence supports:

- immigration questioning at arrival;
- political suspicion and hostile press framing;
- cancellations connected with “Bolshevik tendencies” in an archival chronology;
- a completed departure in early February 1923.

It does not yet provide:

- an order of deportation;
- a statutory ground;
- a removal proceeding;
- a forced-removal document;
- a passenger/immigration record classifying the departure as deportation.

Therefore:

```yaml
myth: Yesenin and Duncan were deported from the United States
verdict: unproven
publication_status: HOLD
```

The reader text may say that political suspicion damaged the tour and accompanied their American experience. It may not convert that atmosphere into a legal deportation.

## 5. Work and writing during the US period

The academic corpus also strengthens the work matrix:

- the concept and portions of `Страна Негодяев` existed before America;
- an early version was read in New York in late January 1923;
- a lost early autograph was reportedly handed over before departure;
- the New York book mock-up was given to Yarmolinsky in November 1922;
- later authorial dates and retrospective accounts require correction against the route chronology.

This supports the verdict that the foreign trip was not merely a sequence of scandals while also preventing the opposite myth that every announced translation or publication succeeded.

## 6. New myth candidate

### YES-US-M06 — `Страна Негодяев` was written from zero in America

```yaml
verdict: false / oversimplified
publication_status: SOURCE-MAPPED
```

Academic comments document the concept and work before the US trip, including the March 1922 letter, the May Berlin autobiography and June press/readings. America was a later development stage with a January 1923 reading and reported lost autograph, not the point of origin.
