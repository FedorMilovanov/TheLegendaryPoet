## 1. Narrative purpose

The year 1924 is not merely the preface to the final year. It is a major structural transition:

- repeated legal/medical instability continues after 1923;
- the poet works intensively between Moscow and Leningrad;
- `Москва кабацкая` finally becomes a physical book after several failed projects and censorship changes;
- the public claim that the Imagist group was “dissolved” triggers immediate dispute rather than a clean organisational ending;
- on 3 September Yesenin leaves for a long Caucasus period;
- correspondence turns Galina Benislavskaya into a practical editorial centre in Moscow;
- `Русь советская`, `Рябиновый костер` plans and the first `Персидские мотивы` emerge;
- a desire to reach Persia or Constantinople remains a documented plan, not a completed route.

## 2. Status vocabulary

- `DOCUMENTED` — dated letter, legal/medical paper, poster, book, inscription or publication.
- `ACADEMICALLY-ESTABLISHED` — academic comments reconstruct the event from named evidence.
- `MEMOIR-MAPPED` — a dated/identified recollection, not stenography.
- `PUBLIC-DECLARATION / DISPUTED EFFECT` — the statement is real, but its claimed institutional result was contested.
- `PLANNED-NOT-COMPLETED` — intention is documented; completion is not.
- `DATE-RANGE` — secure interval without exact day.
- `HOLD` — evidence incomplete for reader-level certainty.

## 5. The 31 August declaration is not a magical institutional switch

```yaml
claim: Imagism ended everywhere on 31 August 1924
verdict: false / reductive
```

What is documented:

- Yesenin and Gruzinov published a letter declaring the group dissolved;
- other Moscow Imagists rejected the claim on 9 September;
- the Leningrad group publicly said Imagism continued;
- Yesenin's personal and publishing connections did not vanish at once.

Safe formula:

> On 31 August Yesenin publicly attempted to end the Moscow group's old configuration, but the authority and effect of the declaration were immediately disputed. The personal, publishing and regional networks dissolved unevenly.

## 6. Persia boundary

## 8. Standing anti-regression rules from 1924

1. **A memoir month can be false.** February Leningrad is rejected by the stronger Moscow hospital chronology.
2. **Hospitalisation proves treatment, not a complete diagnosis.** Do not reconstruct a case history from comments alone.
3. **A book may appear after several failed editions.** Keep GUM, author edition and physical Leningrad `Москва кабацкая` separate.
4. **Contents may preserve censored ghosts.** The 1924 book's contents list names poems removed from the body.
5. **A public dissolution letter has contested effect.** Group networks do not disappear by declaration.
6. **A travel plan is not a visited country.** Tehran/Tabriz/Constantinople remain plan/failed-attempt categories.
7. **A cycle title can precede completion.** `Персидские мотивы` is named in October 1924 but completed later.
8. **Delegation is not abdication.** Benislavskaya had wide authority while Yesenin still issued detailed instructions.
9. **Authorial excitement is dated evidence, not final judgement.** Statements that `Цветы` was his best work describe that moment.
10. **A first publication and a book publication are different events.** `Русь советская` appeared in periodicals before the December book.
