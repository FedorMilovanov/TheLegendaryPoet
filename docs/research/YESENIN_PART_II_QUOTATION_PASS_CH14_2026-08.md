## 1. Selection rule

Chapter 14 is built from unlike evidence classes: autograph letters, telegram blanks, an editorial contract, calendar transcription, bank operations, a clinic certificate and a still-incomplete medical archive. Direct quotation is allowed only when the wording itself changes the claim.

The chapter does not quote:

- intimate notes merely for emotional colour;
- recipient annotations as if they were Yesenin's words;
- certificate no. 1037 as though it were a diagnosis;
- memoir dialogue when the original document exists;
- bank amounts as a psychological confession.

## 4. Other no-quote decisions

- The July notes to Sofia Tolstaya remain documentary examples with provenance and date qualifiers; their intimate wording is unnecessary.
- Sofia's reverse-side annotation about intoxication is described as the recipient's annotation, not quoted for dramatic effect.
- The 30 June contract is summarised by number, date, rights and ten-thousand-line scope; its boilerplate is not quoted.
- Bankbook figures remain documentary values, not a voice or confession.
- The 16 December Ehrlich reply is paraphrased until its exact primary text/provenance is pinned separately.
- Memoir explanations of why treatment began are not quoted as medical conclusions.

## 5. Moral, medical and theological boundaries

The selected documents establish practical action during crisis. They do not prove:

- recovery or hopelessness;
- one motive for marriage;
- one motive for treatment;
- a complete psychiatric diagnosis;
- absence or presence of final repentance;
- that future plans cancel documented instability;
- that the clinic was only legal shelter.
