# Сергей Есенин. Часть II — authoring packet, chapter 1

**Chapter:** `1921: fame, group machinery and an inner fault line`  
**Pass:** 01  
**Target reader text:** 650–800 words  
**Status:** `PACKET READY / EXACT CHRONICLE PAGES STILL TO PIN`  
**Public route:** do not create

## 1. Chapter function

The chapter must establish that the late biography begins before Duncan.

By autumn 1921 Yesenin was already:

- a known author with new and planned books;
- a public reader and group organiser;
- involved in Imagist publishing/performance machinery;
- working on the major dramatic poem `Пугачев`;
- repeatedly testing the boundary between artistic role and destructive conduct;
- legally separated from Zinaida Reich only after the probable Duncan meeting.

The chapter ends with Duncan entering an already active and unstable system.

## 2. Governing claims

- `YES2-C001` — Duncan did not create the entire late crisis.
- `YES2-C002` — the first meeting is probably 3 October, not an uncontested exact day.
- `YES2-W006` — `Пугачев` is the creative spine of 1921.
- `YES2-I001` — Imagism supplied infrastructure but did not invent Yesenin's whole poetics.
- `YES2-I005` — the hooligan role had market value but was not only marketing.

## 3. Opening choice

### Preferred documentary opening

Open with the physical/published 12 June 1921 leaflet `Всеобщая мобилизация` or the 6 August `Пугачев` evening.

The opening must show:

- rhetoric of mobilisation and public art;
- organised group machinery;
- the poet as performer/author, not a passive “discovery”;
- a dated object that can be captioned and sourced.

### Do not open with

- the first glance at Duncan;
- an invented smoky cafe scene;
- a retrospective hotel-death shadow;
- generic “Russia was collapsing” exposition;
- the statement that Yesenin was already inevitably doomed.

## 4. Paragraph-by-paragraph packet

### Paragraph 1 — public machinery

**Purpose:** place the reader inside the 1921 literary/public environment.

**Core sentence:**

> By 1921 Yesenin was not simply a lyrical voice from the village: he worked inside a system of evenings, leaflets, cafes, small publishers and deliberately provocative collective acts.

**Evidence:**

- `Всеобщая мобилизация`, 12 June 1921;
- current Imagism source gate;
- exact poster/programme when acquired.

**Boundary:**

Do not claim that every group slogan expressed Yesenin's stable personal poetics.

### Paragraph 2 — books and unrealised projects

**Purpose:** show ambition and commercial/authorial planning.

**Facts:**

- `Трерядница` appears at the beginning of February;
- it advertises the two-volume `Ржаные кони`;
- `Звездный бык` with Kusikov is in circulation before 26 February;
- `Ржаные кони` remains unrealised.

**Claim IDs:** `YES2-W006`, general plan/book anti-regression.

**Safe formula:**

> The advertisements show a real programme of expansion, not a completed bibliography.

### Paragraph 3 — the 26 February self-description

**Purpose:** give Yesenin's own voice without making it neutral authority.

**Facts:**

- Ivan Rozanov records an oral autobiography on 26 February;
- Yesenin speaks of influences, continuity and severe internal breaks;
- the record differs in places from later autobiographies.

**Source:**

`https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/e77/e77-3432.htm?cmd=p`

**Boundary:**

Name Rozanov as recorder. Do not put reconstructed first-person quotation marks around paraphrase.

### Paragraph 4 — failed foreign-travel plan

**Purpose:** prevent the Duncan trip from appearing as the first thought of going abroad.

**Facts:**

- 2 April: Lunacharsky signs a petition concerning foreign travel for Yesenin and Rurik Ivnev;
- the request did not produce the journey.

**Source:**

`https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/e72/e72-266-.htm?cmd=p`

**Safe formula:**

> A documented plan existed; the planned journey did not.

**Anti-regression:** travel petition ≠ completed travel.

### Paragraph 5 — Turkestan and `Пугачев`

**Purpose:** move from group publicity to sustained creative labour.

**Facts:**

- mid-April departure through Samara/Orenburg toward Turkestan;
- exact 5 May Tashkent contact through dated inscriptions;
- unfinished `Пугачев` read in Tashkent;
- work continues after return before 10 June.

**Sources:**

- `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/es6/es6-233-.htm?cmd=p`;
- `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/es3/es3-435-.htm?cmd=p`.

**Boundary:**

Do not describe a complete tourist itinerary or say the poem was written entirely in Tashkent.

### Paragraph 6 — public readings and theatrical interest

**Purpose:** show `Пугачев` entering serious public/theatrical discussion.

**Facts:**

- June reading before Meyerhold's troupe;
- 6 August public reading at the Literary Mansion;
- August completion;
- December physical publication despite 1922 imprint.

**Sources:**

- PSS comments to `Пугачев`;
- original poster/report page to pin;
- author-book register.

**Safe formula:**

> The work moved from travel manuscript to private reading, public performance and book publication within the year.

### Paragraph 7 — internal difference within Imagism

**Purpose:** prevent the group from becoming a single moral/personality actor.

**Facts:**

- 28 August provocative Block memorial evening;
- mapped evidence says Yesenin reacted sharply against anti-Block speeches;
- 12 September Yesenin–Mariengof manifesto;
- mid-September collective polemic with Lunacharsky.

**Sources:**

- `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/critics/ev1/ev1-445-.htm?cmd=p`;
- collective-document comments.

**Boundary:**

Do not attribute the offensive speeches to Yesenin merely because the evening was organised under the Imagist label.

### Paragraph 8 — probable meeting with Duncan

**Purpose:** introduce the relationship only after the 1921 causal foundation exists.

**Facts:**

- probable 3 October meeting in Yakulov's studio;
- exact-day qualification retained;
- full witness analysis belongs to the independent article.

**Claim ID:** `YES2-C002`.

**Safe transition:**

> When Duncan entered this world in early October, she met not an unknown peasant youth but a famous poet already surrounded by public machinery, unfinished ambitions and serious disorder.

### Paragraph 9 — legal/family boundary

**Purpose:** preserve the Zinaida Reich/children/legal sequence without melodrama.

**Facts:**

- 5 October divorce decision;
- children remain with their mother;
- surviving text is copy-of-copy, original not inspected.

**Source:**

`https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/e72/e72-266-.htm?cmd=p`

**Boundary:**

Do not write an invented confrontation or claim the new relationship caused the legal decision without chronology of proceedings.

### Paragraph 10 — poster/attendance warning

**Purpose:** insert one methodological example into the narrative without breaking flow.

**Facts:**

- 17 October poster announces Yesenin;
- diary evidence places him reading elsewhere.

**Safe reader phrasing:**

> The episode is a useful warning for the whole biography: an old poster proves what organisers promised, not necessarily who appeared.

**Function:**

This sentence prepares the reader to understand later tour and cafe evidence.

### Paragraph 11 — late-year public role

**Purpose:** close the year before Duncan becomes the organising subject.

**Facts:**

- 8 November acquaintance with Schneider;
- 15 November dedicated Yesenin evening at `Стойло Пегаса`;
- December physical `Пугачов` release.

**Boundary:**

An advertised evening does not supply exact poems or behaviour without a report.

### Paragraph 12 — chapter conclusion

**Required synthesis:**

- 1921 combines group promotion, serious work, legal/family transition and public performance;
- the hooligan role is neither pure fabrication nor the whole person;
- Duncan joins the story after these structures exist.

**Bridge to chapter 2:**

> The next question is therefore not how a foreign dancer “created” the late Yesenin, but what happened when two already famous and carefully staged public personalities entered the same Moscow room.
