## 1. Chapter function

The foreign route chapters create a recurring risk: the reader may assume that every book printed in Berlin, every poem carrying a foreign place/date, every announced translation and every work about America was written on location.

This chapter interrupts chronology to classify the evidence.

The chapter must answer five different questions:

1. What existed before departure?
2. What was written or substantially reworked abroad?
3. What was printed abroad from earlier work?
4. What was planned but not completed?
5. What was written after return from foreign impressions?

## 2. Governing claims

- `YES2-W001` — `Страна Негодяев` did not originate from zero in America.
- `YES2-W002` — Berlin publication does not imply Berlin composition.
- `YES2-W003` — announced translations require item-by-item verification.
- `YES2-W004` — the Yarmolinsky project is documented, completion/publication is not.
- `YES2-W005` — `Железный Миргород` is post-return prose from American experience.

## 4. Paragraph packet

### Paragraph 1 — the category error

**Opening:**

> A foreign title page, a city under a poem and an announced translation are three different kinds of evidence.

Explain that publication geography, composition geography and subject geography must be separated.

### Paragraph 2 — `Страна Негодяев` before America

**Facts:**

- March 1922 letter/project evidence;
- May Berlin autobiography refers to the work;
- Berlin public discussion/readings before the US journey;
- late-January New York reading is a development stage.

**Sources:**

- `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/es3/es3-435-.htm?cmd=p`;
- US day-level pass.

**Safe formula:**

> America supplied a later working environment and public reading, not the point of origin.

### Paragraph 3 — Berlin volume

**Facts:**

- 18 May transaction with Grzhebin;
- physical volume appears in October–November while Yesenin is in the US;
- contents mostly predate travel;
- academic comments suggest he probably did not see the final proof.

**Sources:**

- `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/es2/es2-255-.htm?cmd=p`;
- `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/es7/es7-0724.htm?cmd=2`.

**Boundary:**

Do not phrase `Berlin poems` unless referring to publication context, not creation.

### Paragraph 4 — autobiography as primary but interested evidence

**Facts:**

- dated Berlin, 14 May 1922;
- authorial self-compression and geographic generalisation;
- later autobiographies differ.

**Safe formula:**

> The autobiography is invaluable as what Yesenin wanted to say about himself in Berlin; it is not a passport ledger.

### Paragraph 5 — translation projects

**Purpose:** prevent inflated international-success narrative.

Separate:

- published translation;
- contract/commission;
- announced translation;
- translator's work in progress;
- unverified press promise.

**Draft rule:**

Mention only translations whose physical publication or exact institutional item is pinned. Summarise the rest as plans.

### Paragraph 6 — Yarmolinsky project

**Facts:**

- New York mock-up/title object handed to Avrahm Yarmolinsky in November 1922;
- intended English translation/publication;
- surviving academic evidence does not establish completion.

**Source:**

`https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/es7/es7-0724.htm?cmd=2`

**Safe formula:**

> It proves a serious publication attempt, not the existence of an English edition.

### Paragraph 7 — `Железный Миргород`

**Facts:**

- American subject/experience;
- autograph dated Moscow, 14 August 1923;
- two-stage newspaper publication;
- manuscript and newspaper text differ.

**Sources:**

- `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/e75/e75-159-.htm`;
- `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/e75/e75-325-.htm?cmd=p`.

**Safe formula:**

> The essay is evidence of how the journey was transformed after return, not a neutral notebook carried through America.

### Paragraph 8 — lyric dates and the diary fallacy

Explain:

- a place/date under a poem can identify a copy, revision, inscription or authorial dating;
- lyrical first person is not automatically biographical testimony;
- later book arrangement can alter perceived sequence.

Route to chapter 13 for detailed method.

### Paragraph 9 — synthesis

Required conclusion:

> The foreign years were neither creatively empty nor a parade of instant international triumphs. They produced real work, real books and real negotiations — alongside unfinished translations, abandoned volumes and projects known only from letters.
