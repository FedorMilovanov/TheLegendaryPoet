## Controlling statements

1. The later academic PSS name index records **14 known letters** from G. A. Benislavskaya to S. A. Yesenin.
2. The open `Есенин.ру` publication contains **13 numbered units**, and one of those units explicitly identifies itself as a postcard.
3. P. F. Yushin's 1969 book contains a numbered archival-document sequence for Benislavskaya → Yesenin; a direct holder scan of printed p. **411** now verifies positions **#121–#134** in that sequence.
4. The heading immediately above the sequence on p. 411 is **`Архивные документы, использованные или учтенные в книге`**. The five formerly disputed old-date positions are therefore historically attested by Yushin as **archival letter records**, not merely dates copied from an unidentified secondary list.
5. Yushin p. 411 does **not** print an individual archive shelfmark, leaf number, incipit, autograph/copy designation, or later editorial identity for those entries. The page proves the 1969 archival-record layer and its labels; it does not by itself prove modern canonical dating or physical identity.
6. Direct holder inspection of the 1995 volume independently places 4 Mar 1924 on pp. **234–235**, 6 Apr on p. **236**, and 26 Apr on p. **238**, while apparently exposing no item under the old 18-Jan / 8-Feb headings.
7. Naumov's 1970 typed-copy publication layer has directly demonstrable same-text dating instability. Old exact dates must therefore be preserved as evidence about an editorial/copy layer, not automatically promoted to authorial dates.
8. The 16 Jul 1925 branch is independently page-verified by IMLI 2013, pp. **339–340**; transmission to Yesenin remains unproved.
9. The project keeps `13 numbered units ↔ 16 Yushin archival positions ↔ 14 later academic letters` as a reconciliation problem involving identity, dating, document class, editorial inclusion, and provenance.

## RGALI / typed-copy provenance boundary

The modern RGALI fund card for fund 190 independently describes:

`Переписка С. А. Есенина и Г. А. Бениславской — маш. коп. 35 (1921–1925)`.

This is evidence for a **typed-copy correspondence layer**. It is not a count of 35 Benislavskaya → Yesenin letters and it is not evidence that every old date came from an autograph.

Naumov states that he knew **10 Benislavskaya → Yesenin letters** in typed copies then held in `ЦГАЛИ, ф. 190, ед. хр. 106`. Published inventory/Royzman evidence points instead toward unit **105** for Benislavskaya correspondence. The exact official item cards for 105/106 have not yet been directly read here, so the reason for the discrepancy remains open.

## Critical document-class finding

The open publication's unit #4 explicitly says:

`Эта открытка вместо сдачи.`

Therefore **open numbered unit ≠ automatically a letter**. A simple calculation such as `13 units − 1 postcard + selected extras = 14 letters` is rejected.

## Naumov 1970 typed-copy layer — text identity is stronger than exact old dates

Controlled examples:

- Naumov dates one late-April textual object **2 Apr 1924**; the same distinctive text is later published under heading **26 Apr 1924** with internal signature `25.IV.24`.
- Naumov separately treats the **8 Feb 1924** text and the **6 Apr 1924** text; they must not be merged merely to solve the count.
- A January 1925 textual object is referred to in the early layer as **1 Jan**, while the later complete document carries the internal date `20.I.25`.
- The paragraph beginning `У меня тоже деловое настроение. Занимаюсь. Ваши стихи в порядок привожу...` is assigned to **1 Dec 1924** by Naumov and repeated as such in 1986 commentary, while the later 1995/open/PSS layer places the same identifying paragraph inside the **15 Dec 1924** letter.
- Naumov says Benislavskaya wrote to Yesenin in December `трижды`, while his article exposes a more complicated set of December date references.

Therefore existence of a historical textual/archival record and correctness of its old exact date are separate questions.

## 30 November / 1 December / 15 December — record existence closed, identity still open

The new p. 411 scan changes one part of this node and leaves another part open:

1. **Closed:** Yushin really does carry #128 as an archival letter record dated **30 Nov 1924**.
2. **Closed:** Naumov and 1986 commentary assign the identifying `деловое настроение / стихи в порядок привожу` paragraph to **1 Dec**.
3. **Closed:** the later open/1995/PSS layer places that same identifying paragraph under **15 Dec**.
4. **Open:** p. 411 gives no incipit, source shelfmark or textual boundary for #128, so it does not prove that Yushin #128 is physically identical to the Naumov/15-Dec text.

The correct model remains a **30 Nov / 1 Dec / 15 Dec date-layer conflict**, but the existence of the Yushin 30-Nov archival-record layer is now direct evidence rather than an unread bibliographic assumption.

## 29 December 1924 — old archival record versus late exchange model

Yushin p. 411 directly proves the existence of an archival-letter record labelled **29 Dec 1924**. That materially strengthens the historical reality of the old record.

The late PSS nevertheless reconstructs the relevant Batumi reply exchange using **25 and 27 Dec**. Therefore the unresolved question is no longer whether Yushin actually listed a 29-Dec letter record; he did. The unresolved question is **what physical/textual object that old archival record represented and why the later corpus does not retain it as a separate 29-Dec reply**.

No merge with 27 Dec is authorized without a shared incipit, source shelfmark or editorial redating note.

## 12 April 1925 — old archival record versus late response chronology

Yushin p. 411 directly proves an archival-letter record labelled **12 Apr 1925**.

Later PSS commentary to Yesenin's 8-Apr letter says Benislavskaya wrote her answer **only on 4 May**, because she was in Konstantinovo for Easter. The later 1995 page topology also places other correspondents between the 9-Feb and 4-May Benislavskaya items.

Therefore the node is a genuine **old archival-record versus later editorial chronology conflict**. The old record may represent a superseded date, a different document identity/classification, or another archival-copy problem. The evidence does not support declaring the record fictional.

The earlier Flerovsky/Persia shortcut remains rejected: that passage belongs to the 4-May text.

## 16 July 1925 — page witness closed, transmission open

The editors say the archive location cited by Zankovskaya is erroneous and that the letter remained with Benislavskaya.

Consequences:
