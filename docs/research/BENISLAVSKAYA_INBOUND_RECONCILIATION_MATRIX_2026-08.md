## Critical document-class finding

The open publication's unit #4 explicitly says:

`Эта открытка вместо сдачи.`

Therefore **open numbered unit ≠ letter**. The open page contains a postcard inside its 13-unit numbering.

A tempting shortcut — `13 units − 1 postcard + 1 Dec + 16 Jul = 14 letters` — remains rejected. The independently attested text beginning `Бросьте эту пьяную канитель`, assigned to 8 Feb in the older Naumov/Yushin layer, is also absent from the open 13-unit page and now conflicts with direct holder inspection of the later 1995 volume under that old date.

At least one unresolved node therefore still requires **redating, physical identity/duplication, editorial inclusion criteria, or document-boundary/classification**.

## Naumov 1970 typed-copy layer — text identity is stronger than its exact dates

Naumov states that he knew **10 Benislavskaya→Yesenin letters** in typed copies then held in `ЦГАЛИ, ф. 190, ед. хр. 106`. His article can be mapped to ten unique textual objects once one proven duplicate-by-misdating is collapsed:

1. 8 Feb 1924;
2. an object Naumov dates **2 Apr 1924**, later published as the same text under heading **26 Apr 1924** with internal signature **`25.IV.24`**;
3. 6 Apr 1924 — a separate text from the 8-Feb object;
4. 28 Apr 1924;
5. 1 Dec 1924;
6. 15 Dec 1924;
7. 25 Dec 1924;
8. 27 Dec 1924;
9. 20 Jan 1925 — the same textual object Naumov elsewhere refers to as **1 Jan 1925**;
10. 4 May 1925.

Two same-text date failures/revisions are directly demonstrable:

- `NAUMOV 02-APR-1924 → SAME TEXT / LATER HEADING 26-APR-1924 / INTERNAL DATE 25-IV-24`;
- `NAUMOV 01-JAN-1925 → SAME TEXT / INTERNAL DATE 20-I-25`.

The second identity is controlled by the distinctive shared sentence about A. A. Berzin and the news that Yesenin had married; the later complete document carries the internal date `20.I.25`.

Consequences:

- the reality of a textual object and the correctness of its old exact date are separate questions;
- the 8-Feb text remains real and separate from 6 Apr, but its exact old date is materially weaker as modern dating evidence than it would be in a stable publication layer;
- 18 Jan 1924, 29 Dec 1924 and 12 Apr 1925 are **not represented in this ten-object article map** and are therefore `YUSHIN-EXTRA RELATIVE TO NAUMOV TEN-COPY WITNESS`;
- this does **not** prove those Yushin positions fictional: Yushin may have used additional sources, different copy labels or different editorial inclusion criteria;
- Naumov also says Benislavskaya wrote to Yesenin in December `трижды`, while the article separately exposes 1 Dec plus 15/25/27-Dec references. That internal tension must not be silently harmonized.

This is an **article-level textual map**, not a transcription of an archival inventory.

## Vdovin 1977 — controlled pagination and access boundary

The official *Вопросы литературы* archive identifies Vdovin's article as **pp. 233–248**, which controls the full article extent.

Later academic Esenin commentary cites **pp. 236–246** while discussing the Benislavskaya / `Песнь о великом походе` material and explicitly states that Vdovin publishes the Benislavskaya→Yesenin letter of **10 Oct 1924**.

Therefore:

- `233–248` = official full article span;
- `236–246` = academically cited high-value internal range, not the whole article;
- abbreviated secondary references such as `234–235` must not replace the official full extent;
- the public journal archive preview does not provide a lawful complete free read, so this route remains a discovery/textological-control route unless an equivalent lawful copy is found.

- The inspected 1995 volume places 4 Mar on pp. 234–235, 6 Apr on p. 236 and 26 Apr on p. 238.
- The inspected 1995 volume apparently does not carry letters under the old dates 18 Jan and 8 Feb 1924.
- This absence-under-date does **not** prove the older-position texts nonexistent.

- Naumov's ten-object article map does not expose separate 18-Jan, 29-Dec or 12-Apr objects; these are Yushin-extra relative to that witness, not thereby disproved.
- 1 Dec 1924 is an academically identified Benislavskaya→Yesenin letter outside the open 13-unit page.
- `Бросьте эту пьяную канитель` is academically attested as a Benislavskaya→Yesenin 1924 letter; its older 8-Feb dating/later-edition identity is unresolved.
