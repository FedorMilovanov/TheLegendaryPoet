## 1. Critical correction: title-page date is not release date

A common formula states that the Imagist Declaration first appeared in the Voronezh journal `Сирена`, no. 4–5, dated 30 January 1919, and was then repeated in the Moscow newspaper `Советская страна`, no. 3, on 10 February.

The academic `Летопись жизни и творчества С. А. Есенина` establishes a more complicated sequence:

- the `Сирена` no. 4–5 title page carries the date 30 January 1919;
- the issue was probably typeset or substantially prepared by that date;
- because of production delays, the issue actually appeared only on 17 or 18 April 1919;
- `Советская страна`, no. 3, physically appeared on 10 February 1919;
- therefore the newspaper was the first released publication of the Declaration;
- the newspaper and journal texts are not identical, and the newspaper shows later textual changes.

Academic chronology:

- `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/el-abc/el2/el2-199-.htm?cmd=p&istext=1`

### Safe public formula

> The Declaration was prepared for `Сирена` no. 4–5, whose title page bears 30 January 1919, but production delays held the issue until April. The first publication to reach readers was `Советская страна` no. 3 on 10 February; the two texts contain variants.

### Forbidden simplified formulas

- `The Declaration was first published in Sirena on 30 January 1919.`
- `Soviet Country merely reprinted the Sirena text.`
- `The two witnesses are textually identical.`

## 2. Why both witnesses are required

The academic chronology says the newspaper version contains later editing, including punctuation and wording changes. Therefore the article needs both physical witnesses:

1. `Сирена`, Voronezh, 1919, no. 4–5, Declaration at columns/pages 48–50;
2. `Советская страна`, Moscow, 10 February 1919, no. 3.

A later academic transcription can support reader text, but it cannot replace a visual and textual comparison of the first witnesses when the article specifically discusses publication priority and variants.
