## 1. Chapter function

The biography must not let documents, arrests and relationships swallow the poetry.

At the same time, the chapter must refuse three common errors:

1. every first-person line is direct autobiography;
2. every dark image is a psychiatric symptom;
3. every religious or penitential phrase proves repentance or conversion.

The selected texts should show formal control, historical reflection, self-accusation, imagined geography and long composition — not one undifferentiated “voice of collapse”.

## 3. Required analytical method for every poem

```text
1. exact text witness and date
2. publication/book history
3. speaker, genre and composition
4. biographical/historical context
5. what the text illuminates
6. what the text cannot prove alone
7. moral/theological conclusion only if supported
```

No poem enters the chapter without all seven fields.

## 4. Proposed text set

### A. `Русь советская`

**Function:** historical/political self-location after return and during the Caucasus period.

**Evidence:**

- periodical publication in 1924;
- physical book/publication history;
- dated Caucasus performance/reception;
- relationship to `Русь уходящая` and the broader late-Russia sequence.

**Source base:**

- `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/es1/es1-385-.htm?cmd=p`;
- 1924 chronology;
- future physical `Русь советская`/`Страна Советская` edition.

**What it illuminates:**

- estrangement from the new social order;
- refusal to pretend complete belonging;
- persistence of attachment to Russia despite displacement;
- tension between public identity and historical change.

**What it cannot prove:**

- one final fixed political position for all later months;
- literal documentary accuracy of every village scene;
- Christian repentance from religious/cultural language alone.

### B. `Письмо к женщине`

**Function:** crafted retrospective address, guilt and historical self-interpretation.

**Evidence required:**

- exact autograph/publication date;
- addressee/source history;
- relation to Zinaida Reich without treating the poem as court testimony;
- variant and book-placement evidence.

**What it illuminates:**

- the poetic reconstruction of rupture and historical disorientation;
- acknowledgement of harm and self-justification in the same rhetorical act;
- the difference between retrospective understanding and repaired relationship.

**What it cannot prove:**

- exact chronology of the marriage;
- that every accusation or explanation is factually neutral;
- repentance merely because guilt is voiced;
- the full private response of the addressee.

### C. `Персидские мотивы` — two-poem pairing

Suggested pair:

- one early poem from October 1924–January 1925;
- one of the final four Baku/Mardakan poems from August 1925.

**Function:** show development of a cycle rather than quote one famous line as the whole project.

**Evidence:**

- first six poems: October 1924–January 1925;
- two more in Moscow, March 1925;
- two more in Baku, April;
- late-May author book;
- final four in August;
- literary Persian sources and stories from people who knew the region.

**What it illuminates:**

- imagined geography as a form of distance from Moscow/Russia;
- stylised dialogue, colour and lyric role;
- desire for another emotional order;
- formal transformation across the cycle.

**What it cannot prove:**

- physical presence in Persia;
- literal Shagane biography in every line;
- stable recovery or fulfilled love;
- identity between May book and final complete cycle.

### D. `Анна Снегина`

**Function:** sustained narrative intelligence and reorganisation of autobiographical material.

**Evidence:**

- completion in Batum, January 1925;
- periodical publication;
- planned standalone book unrealised;
- historical/village source matrix;
- composite/reshaped chronology.

**What it illuminates:**

- ability to transform personal memory into narrative distance;
- village revolution and loss through multiple voices;
- the difference between lyric confession and narrative composition;
- continued large-form work during a difficult period.

**What it cannot prove:**

- literal identity of characters with one real person;
- exact diary chronology of 1917–1918;
- that narrative control equals personal stability;
- every political conclusion as direct authorial statement.

### E. `Черный человек`

**Function:** climax of the chapter, but not a diagnostic chart or automatic suicide note.

**Evidence:**

- project/work history reaches back to 1923 or earlier;
- unfinished version read in Leningrad 3–6 November 1925;
- intensive revision 12–14 November;
- final text completed 14 November, before clinic admission;
- Sofia Tolstaya's witness and textual record.

**Source base:**

- `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/es3/es3-188-.htm?cmd=p`;
- `https://feb-web.ru/feb/esenin/texts/es3/es3-435-.htm?cmd=p`;
- Jan–Nov 1925 chronology.

**What it illuminates:**

- self-division and hostile internal dialogue as literary construction;
- long deliberate work rather than spontaneous final outburst;
- memory, shame, public image and accusation;
- the capacity to shape crisis formally.

**What it cannot prove:**

- a specific psychiatric diagnosis;
- that every figure/episode is literal autobiography;
- that it was written in the clinic;
- exact intent regarding December death;
- conversion or repentance merely from moral terror.

### F. Optional closing clinic poem: `Клен ты мой опавший...`

**Function:** close with late winter imagery without turning it into prophecy.

**Evidence:**

- clinic-period creation after 26 November;
- window/garden context preserved through Sofia's records/comments;
- manuscript/date source required.

**What it illuminates:**

- continued creativity in treatment;
- transformation of visible winter into self-address;
- tenderness and self-recognition alongside instability.

**What it cannot prove:**

- a discharge plan;
- exact clinical state;
- premeditated death;
- literal identity between tree and author.

## 6. Moral and theological boundary

### Allowed conclusion

> The late poems repeatedly name waste, estrangement, guilt and self-deception. That moral clarity matters; it does not by itself document the repentance and durable turn that the life records would need to show.

### Do not write

- `He repented through poetry.`
- `The poems prove he died a believer.`
- `The black man is a demon who literally visited him.`
- `The poem proves schizophrenia/depression/psychosis.`
- `His genius redeemed his life.`

### Required sequence

Literary analysis first. Moral/theological evaluation only after the text's genre and evidence limits are clear.
