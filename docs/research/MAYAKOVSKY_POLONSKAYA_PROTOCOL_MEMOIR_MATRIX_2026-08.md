## 3. Major contradiction: nature of the relationship

### 1930 protocol

The protocol states that Polonskaya did not love him, would not live with him or leave her husband, and explicitly records that there was no sexual relationship despite his insistence.

### 1938 memoir

The memoir describes mutual love and intimacy, says she was pregnant by him and underwent an abortion, and later asserts that the question of living with him had in principle been decided for her.

### Verdict

```yaml
claim: The 1930 protocol gives a complete truthful description of the intimate relationship.
verdict: false / incomplete
reason: materially contradicted by Polonskaya's later detailed admission; protocol context included marriage, public scandal and official questioning
```

```yaml
claim: The 1938 memoir can simply replace the 1930 statement.
verdict: false
reason: it was written eight years later, acknowledges memory gaps and retrospectively interprets responsibility and causation
```

Safe formula:

> In the official statement of 14 April Polonskaya minimised or denied the intimacy and mutuality later described in her 1938 memoir. The two texts must be read as different statements produced under different pressures, not as interchangeable transcripts.

## 4. Major contradiction: her decision on 13–14 April

### 1930 protocol

The official account says she told him that she did not love him, would not live with him and would not leave Yanshin.

### 1938 memoir

The later account says she loved Mayakovsky and would eventually have lived with him, but was frightened by immediate ultimatums: abandon the theatre, leave her husband at once and remain locked in the room.

### Editorial boundary

The later memoir may reveal what Polonskaya believed in 1938 about her intentions in 1930. It does not prove what Mayakovsky knew, what she would actually have done, or that agreement at that moment would have prevented death.

Forbidden shortcut:

- `She rejected him because she did not love him.`
- `She had already certainly chosen him and only a misunderstanding caused the death.`

Safe formula:

> The early protocol records a categorical refusal; the later memoir reframes it as refusal of an immediate ultimatum rather than absence of love. The difference is itself historically important.

## 5. Major contradiction: causal explanation

### 1930 protocol

Polonskaya says the cause was unknown to her, then offers a supposition combining:

- her refusal of reciprocity;
- the failure of `Баня`;
- a nervous and painful condition.

The wording is already an inference, not an expert diagnosis.

### 1938 memoir

The memoir builds a stronger retrospective theory:

- Mayakovsky's tendency to magnify events;
- their morning quarrel becoming a total break in his perception;
- her inability to find the right response;
- a `misunderstanding` leading to catastrophe.

### Verdict

Neither text establishes one cause. The memoir's final causal confidence is stronger than its evidence and appears after eight years of grief, responsibility and self-reinterpretation.

Safe formula:

> Polonskaya's earliest official account named several possible pressures and admitted that the cause was unknown. Her 1938 memoir gave the quarrel and his emotional maximalism a much larger causal role. The later explanation is evidence of her retrospective understanding, not a forensic conclusion.

Forbidden:

- `Polonskaya caused the suicide.`
- `The failure of Баня caused the suicide.`
- `A single misunderstanding caused the suicide.`
- `The protocol establishes a psychiatric diagnosis.`

## 7. Exhibition and literary-isolation correction

The memoir contains two statements that must remain together:

- many young visitors attended and asked questions;
- Polonskaya reports Mayakovsky's complaint that no writer came.

Therefore it supports neither `the exhibition was empty` nor `there was no professional isolation`. The distinction is:

```text
public/youth attendance
≠ attendance or support by literary organisations and peers
```

This aligns with the current project myth correction and must not be compressed back into the false phrase `никто не пришёл`.

## 8. Brik-related testimony

Polonskaya's memoir describes Lilya Brik as caring, demanding and at times despotic, and reports later conversations after Mayakovsky's death. These are:

- Polonskaya's observations and recollections;
- not proof of Lilya's secret motive;
- not evidence of a financial conspiracy;
- not permission to demonise or absolve any participant.

Statements allegedly spoken by Brik after the death require comparison with Brik's own texts and other witnesses.
